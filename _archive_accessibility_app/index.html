San Francisco · What this website does, in one line: it checks if wheelchair ramps that "should" be there actually are

The city's map says there's a ramp here. There isn't.

San Francisco keeps a giant list of every wheelchair ramp it believes exists on every street corner. The city itself admits the list can be wrong, and it only gets updated about once every three months. Grade checks each entry on that list against a real, recent photo of the actual corner — and if the ramp is missing, blocked, or too steep, it tells you, and plans a walking route that avoids it.

The problem

Nobody checks if the ramp list is actually true

The city says this, plainly, about its own ramp list: it's only meant for planning, it might have mistakes, and it only gets updated every few months.

How it works

Five simple steps

STEP 1
Get the city's list

We download every ramp the city says exists in the area you pick — where it is, what kind of ramp it should be, and when the city last checked it.

STEP 2
Find a recent photo of that corner

We search Mapillary — a free service where people upload street photos — for the newest, clearest photo of that same spot.

STEP 3
Look at the photo for the ramp

A computer program looks at the photo and checks: is there a ramp there at all? And is anything blocking it — a parked car, construction, trash, anything?

STEP 4
Check how steep it is

The same photo is used to estimate how steep the ramp is, and compare that against the legal limit (a ramp can't rise more than 1 inch for every 12 inches it runs). A ramp can exist and still be too steep to use safely.

STEP 5
Plan a route around the bad spots

Using everything we just found, we plan two walking routes: the usual shortest way, and one that goes out of its way to avoid every ramp that failed the check. The full list of problems can also be exported as a report.

What you get

A simple list of ramps that need a second look

Worst problems shown first, each one with the photo that proves it. That's the whole point — so someone planning a trip today can avoid the bad corners, and the city can know exactly which corners to send someone out to fix.
